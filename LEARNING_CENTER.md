# Tradysquids Learning Center

Educational information only. Nothing here is professional financial advice or
a promise of profit. Options can lose 100% of premium; short options and spreads
can involve assignment, exercise, pin, liquidity, and expiration risk.

## Start here

1. Learn bid, ask, midpoint, limit orders, volume, and open interest.
2. Learn calls, puts, strike, premium, expiration, DTE, and moneyness.
3. Learn maximum loss, breakeven, position sizing, and total account exposure.
4. Paper trade the entire entry-to-exit process before risking money.
5. Keep a journal and review a meaningful sample, not one lucky result.

## Contract basics

- One standard equity option normally represents 100 shares.
- A $0.45 ask normally means $45 per contract before fees.
- A call buyer generally benefits from an upward move; a put buyer generally
  benefits from a downward move.
- Delta estimates sensitivity to a $1 stock move. Theta estimates time decay.
  Implied volatility reflects priced movement expectations. None is a guarantee.
- A narrow bid/ask spread and meaningful volume/open interest can reduce, but
  never eliminate, fill and slippage risk.

## Defined-risk spreads

A vertical credit spread sells one option and buys another as protection.
Maximum profit is the credit. Maximum loss is generally spread width minus
credit, multiplied by 100. The short leg can be assigned early. Closing before
expiration can avoid some, but not all, expiration and pin risk.

## Reading a setup

Review trend, intraday momentum, support/resistance, volatility, liquidity,
event risk, expiration, maximum loss, target, stop, and invalidation. A cheap
contract is not automatically low risk: it may be illiquid, far out of the
money, or close to expiration.

## Risk process

- Decide maximum dollar loss before entry.
- Use limit orders and do not chase poor fills.
- Treat correlated positions as combined exposure.
- Never risk money needed for bills or emergencies.
- Know whether a position can be exercised or assigned.
- A stopped paper trade is a loss in this system; there is no scratch category.

## Performance and learning

The system records why a setup qualified, entry data, management updates,
outcome, and provider timestamps. Reviews compare strategies, tickers, regimes,
DTE, delta, liquidity, and event context. Results can suggest a human-reviewed
change, but the bot never changes filters by itself and never executes trades.

## Useful commands

- `/ask question:` beginner explanations from a curated local knowledge base.
- `/explain topic:` concise definitions for common options concepts.
- `/quote ticker:` price, spread, volume, and freshness.
- `/trend ticker:` indicators and regime context.
- `/chart ticker:` a current chart.
- `/chain ticker: side:` liquid-contract research, not a recommendation.
- `/risk premium: contracts:` premium exposure and management examples.
- `/filters`: view the active scanner limits.

Always confirm live quotes, contract specifications, and risk in your own
brokerage interface before making an independent decision.
